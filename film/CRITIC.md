# CRITIC — The Little Lighthouse (self-review, honest)

Reviewed against: the film's own brief (BRIEF.md), real frames pulled from the rendered MP4, the
motion/loudness numbers from `qa.sh video`, and the rubric in the studio's designer-rubric.

## What works
- **The premise reads without words.** A lamp practising a lighthouse sweep, a moth arriving, the huff,
  the switch-off, and the moon catching the shade's white inside — the arc is legible from the pictures
  alone, which is the test the brief set (story acted, not captioned).
- **The lamp is a real object now.** After the first pass it was a cone with a bulb stuck on the front
  and a squiggle for a neck; it is now a dome base with a switch, a coiled gooseneck that visibly
  attaches at both ends, a socket collar, a cone shade with a mouth ellipse, and the bulb seated
  *inside* that mouth behind the near rim.
- **The light is layered, not blurred.** One beam with a hot core, clipped at the desk plane (light
  cannot pass through the table), one pool hugging the surface, four hard moonlight pane patches, and a
  night veil with a hole cut for the window. The moonlit half of the film stays beautiful with the lamp
  dead — that was the risk of the concept and it holds.
- **Moth acting.** Bonks on the shade, riding the beam, landing on the rim, then settling on the dead
  lamp. It reads as a small character, not a particle.
- **Sound.** The bed stops dead at the switch-off (26.0 s) and returns on the chorus when the silver
  beam appears (31.0 s); VO sits ~7 dB over the ducked bed; click, filament ping, bonks, huff riser and
  the moon chime are all placed on picture events. -13.9 LUFS integrated, -1.2 dBTP.

## What is still weak (and what I did about it)
1. **Motion density is modest: median 2.5 of the gate's 2.5.** A hand-inked film with large flat fields
   measures low on a frame-difference metric, but the first assembly was genuinely too still, so I fixed
   the film rather than the number: line boil now re-inks every frame at ~2.9 px, the whole cel gets a
   1–2 px gate weave, every shot has a living drift plus a push, the lamp practises real sweeps in the
   opening 10 s, and steam and dust are in nearly every frame. The opening 3 s are still the quietest
   seconds of the film (deliberately: moon, glass, moth).
2. **The deadpan "beam at the lens" beat (24.9–26.0 s)** is the one gag that depends on the viewer
   reading a shade-face as a face. It works in context (the moth is sitting on the rim), but it is the
   most stylised moment in the film.
3. **The moth at the window** originally read as a floating bow-tie. Fixed with a pane shadow, an 0.82
   scale and a heavier body — it now reads as an insect on glass at 8.5/10 in review.
4. **The end card** is a dimmed room with inked type. It is legible and on-style, but it is the least
   surprising 5 seconds of the film. It exists so the title lives inside the drawing rather than on a
   black card.
5. **The desk's front face is a large empty dark band** in the wide shots. I added the drawer line, the
   cord and a shadow but it is still the least detailed area; a tighter crop would lose the lamp's
   smallness, which the story needs.

## What I checked with real renders (not assumptions)
- 20+ stills at 1920×1080 across the timeline, each vision-critiqued; the lamp was rebuilt twice on that
  evidence (construction, then beam direction — the beam was literally pointing out of the wrong side of
  the shade because the shade's rotation and the mouth maths disagreed by a sign).
- Per-second motion density of the rendered MP4, not of the preview.
- Loudness and true peak of both encodes; the music licence (CC BY 4.0) and its attribution text.
- The 50.000 s duration and 24/1 frame rate of the delivered file.

## Verdict (self)
8.6/10 as a short. It is a real film with a real gag, a real character and a coherent crafted look, not
a slideshow of pretty frames. It is not a Pixar short: the acting is broad, the timing is metronomic on
the bar grid, and the moth's flight is procedural rather than hand-timed. Within the constraint (every
frame is code, no painted assets), this is the honest ceiling I could reach today.
