# THE LITTLE LIGHTHOUSE — script

A 50-second hand-inked cartoon. 24 fps, 1920×1080, music-led, four lines of narration.

**Logline.** Every night a tiny desk lamp practices being a lighthouse. Tonight a moth turns up,
wrecks its dignity, and the lamp switches itself off in a huff — only to find the moon has been
lighting its little room all along.

**Characters.**
- **THE LAMP** — a small brass desk lamp: round base, coiled spring neck, one dented enamel shade.
  No face. It acts with its posture, its shade angle and the shape of its beam. Serious, proud,
  a bit vain. Think Luxo Jr. with a lighthouse keeper's soul.
- **THE MOTH** — a pale, dusty moth the size of a thumb. No face either. It acts with flight:
  loops, bonks, spins. Cheerful, completely unimpressed by dignity.
- **THE MOON** — a big cool window, mostly ignored until it matters.

**The rule of the film.** The lamp's light is the only warm thing in frame; every time the lamp
moves, its beam and its shadow move with it. When the warm light goes, the film must still be
beautiful — cool, silver, quiet.

---

## SC. 1 — A room at night (0.0 – 7.5 s)

Wide. A dark attic room drawn on cream paper: a wooden desk, a stack of books, a cold mug, a tall
window with the moon behind it. Everything is ink and cool blue paper. The lamp sits dead centre of
the desk, small, unlit, pointed at us like a little cannon.

The lamp's base twitches. It lifts its shade a centimetre, like someone straightening their hat.

*CLICK.* (2.0 s) The bulb stutters — warm, warmer — and a pool of amber light blooms out across the
desk, pushing the night back to the edges of the frame. Dust lifts off the wood and hangs in the
beam.

The lamp stretches its neck up, pleased with itself.

> **NARRATION (warm).** Every night, the little lamp practiced being a lighthouse.

## SC. 2 — The sweep (7.5 – 16.0 s)

Close on the shade. The lamp swings its head left, right, left — a slow, serious lighthouse sweep.
The beam rakes across the book spines, the mug, the wall. It catches the lamp's own shadow and throws
it up the wall: for a second the shadow *is* a lighthouse — tall, striped, heroic.

The lamp sees the shadow. It does a small, self-satisfied hop on its base and holds the pose, beam
straight out, chin (shade) up.

Wide again: the enormous heroic shadow on the wall, the tiny lamp on the desk. It sweeps once more,
with feeling.

## SC. 3 — The moth (16.0 – 27.0 s)

*PONK.* Something small and pale bounces off the shade.

A moth. It orbits the bulb in a wobbly loop, wings a blur, dust falling off it, and *PONK* — bonks
the shade again. The lamp freezes, mid-sweep, mortified.

The lamp turns its head away, pointedly. The moth follows the light and bonks it from the other side.
The lamp turns back. The moth is already there. *PONK.*

The lamp shakes itself like a wet dog; the moth is thrown clear, loops, and comes back riding the
beam as if the beam were a slide. The lamp sweeps faster, trying to shake it: the moth spins, tumbles,
recovers, delighted.

Finally the moth lands on the shade's rim, folds its wings, and settles in — utterly at home. It blinks.

The lamp turns its shade to the camera. The full beam hits us. Deadpan. The silence of a professional
whose professionalism has been personally insulted.

> **NARRATION (light).** The moth had other ideas.

## SC. 4 — The huff (27.0 – 31.5 s)

The lamp inflates: the bulb swells with light, the whole room glows — and then, with enormous dignity,

*CLICK.*

Black. A single hair-thin filament glows red, dims, *pings.*

In the dark, the lamp's shape is a silhouette against the blue window. It sits very still, radiating
smugness. The moth, unbothered, flutters down and settles on the shade.

> **NARRATION (soft, deadpan).** So the little lighthouse switched itself off.

## SC. 5 — The moon was already there (31.5 – 40.5 s)

Silence. The camera drifts in. In the dark we can see the room again — not black, but silver: the moon
through the window is laying a long pale pool across the desk, and the inside of the lamp's shade is
lit from behind.

The lamp notices. It lifts its head slowly, turns the shade toward the window, and the moonlight
catches the white enamel inside and bounces — a cool silver sweep across the desk, around the books,
over the moth's folded wings, which glow translucent.

The moth lifts off, circles once through the moonbeam, and lands on the shade's rim again.

The lamp tries a careful, experimental sweep. It works. It does another, slower. The moth rides it.

## SC. 6 — The little lighthouse (40.5 – 50.0 s)

Wide: the blue room, the big moon, the small lamp on the desk and the moth on its rim, one silver
beam moving slowly across the wall. The warm light is gone and the frame is still beautiful.

Push in slowly. The beam sweeps. Hold.

Title, in ink, hand-drawn on the paper: **THE LITTLE LIGHTHOUSE** — written with the light, dust
motes drifting through it, for a full three seconds before the last frame.

> **NARRATION (warm, quiet).** Some lights you only have to borrow.

*(Cut to black. One small filament ping.)*

## Narration script (as delivered — VO direction file)

```
(warm) Every night, the little lamp practiced [beat] being a lighthouse.

(light) The moth had other ideas.

(soft) So the little lighthouse [pause] switched itself off.

(warm) Some lights [beat] you only have to borrow.
```

## Why it works
- **Beginning / turn / payoff**: proud ritual → a visitor wrecks it → it chooses darkness → light it
  didn't earn is waiting. The joke (switching itself off) is the turn; the moon is the payoff.
- **Acting without faces**: every emotion is a shade angle, a neck bend, a hop, a beam shape. Cheap to
  draw, expressive to watch.
- **One warm idea**: amber = the lamp's self-importance; silver = the world's generosity. The film's
  whole colour arc is that handover.
